# Sift v2.0.0

Sift has a whole new look, Home can free up space in one go, and a new page finds what AI coding tools leave behind on your disk.

## A new look

- **Every screen is redesigned.** Sift now sits on a soft gradient — mint in light mode, deep teal in dark — with frosted-glass cards, a coloured icon for each page in the sidebar, and motion that springs a little, the way things do in macOS. Lists arrive one row after another, buttons give when you press them, and the first scan and the freeing screen now show a small sifting ball with a ring that fills as Sift works. Teal still means one thing: space you can get back.

- **Calmer if you'd like it to be.** If you've turned on Reduce motion in your system settings, Sift keeps the bounces and drifting to a minimum.

## Home

- **Sweep it all from one place.** Home now lists everything Sift found, and you can tap any item to include it or leave it out. Press **Sweep** and each item ticks off right there, with a running total of what you've got back. You can keep using your computer, or even switch pages, while it works. **Review everything** is still there if you'd rather look item by item first.

- **Sift asks before anything is deleted for good.** Each item says whether it's kept in Restore for 30 days or deleted for good. Emptying the Trash and clearing temporary files can't be undone, so if either is in your sweep, Sift tells you which ones and asks you to confirm before it starts. A sweep that only moves things to Restore starts straight away.

- **Shortcuts to the rest of Sift.** Tiles under the sweep show how much is in duplicate copies and old build output, how many big files are worth a look, and what's waiting in Restore. Tap one to go there.

## AI agents

- **A new page for AI coding tools.** If you use Claude, Cursor, VS Code, Windsurf, Codex or Ollama, they quietly collect things over time: copies of your projects an agent worked in (worktrees), older versions of the tools themselves, caches, old conversations, and saved state for folders you've since deleted. **AI agents**, under Power tools, finds them and shows what each tool is using. On a computer that uses a few of these tools every day, expect a few gigabytes.

- **Careful about your work.** A worktree that still has changes you haven't committed, or work that isn't on any other branch, starts switched off and says why. Sift won't touch a worktree a program is working in, or the version of a tool you're running, and never your settings, sign-ins or projects themselves.

- **Clear about what comes back.** Worktrees, old versions, old conversations and editor state go to Restore for 30 days. App caches are deleted for good — the apps rebuild them — so Sift asks you to confirm first, and the app has to be closed before Sift can clear its caches. Old conversations start switched off.

- **Local models are listed, not removed.** Downloaded models (Ollama) are often the biggest thing on the page, so Sift shows how much space each one takes. To remove one, use the model tool itself.

## Smaller changes

- **Apps shows what you don't use.** New tiles at the top of Apps show apps you haven't opened in six months, all your apps together, and their caches. Tap **Not opened in 6 months** to see just those apps.

- **Clearer about what's permanent.** "Just clear caches" in Apps and cleaning on the Developer page delete those files for good — they always did, and both pages now say so plainly. Uninstalling an app still keeps everything in Restore for 30 days.

- **Updating on Windows.** "Restart to update" could fail with a message that Setup couldn't close Sift, because Sift was still shutting down. Setup now waits a few seconds for Sift to finish closing before it continues.

## One thing worth knowing

Moving a worktree to Restore keeps your files safe, but it doesn't change anything in git. If you bring one back after git has tidied up its list of worktrees, the files return but git may no longer treat that folder as a worktree. Your commits are unaffected either way.

## Works on

- **macOS** 10.15+ (Apple Silicon and Intel)
- **Windows** 10+ (x64 and ARM64)
- **Linux** (x86_64)
