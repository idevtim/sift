# Sift v1.0.0

The first release of Sift: it shows you how much space you can safely get back, explains what it found in plain words, and keeps what it removes for 30 days in case you change your mind.

## Getting space back

- **One number on Home.** Sift checks six things: old installers, the Trash (the Recycle Bin on Windows), leftovers from apps you've already uninstalled, temporary files, duplicate photos and large videos you haven't opened in a year. Home adds them up into how much you can free today. The quick checks land in a few seconds; duplicates and old videos follow once Sift has finished looking through your files.

- **Nothing goes without your OK.** Review explains each category before anything happens. The safe ones start switched on; duplicate photos and old videos start off, because only you know if you still need them. **See all** lists every file behind a category, so you can show one in Finder or Explorer, or remove things one at a time.

- **Restore keeps things for 30 days.** Most of what Sift removes is moved aside, not deleted. Open Restore and put anything back in one click. After 30 days it's cleared on its own, or sooner if you choose **Clear now** — that one asks you to click twice, because it can't be undone.

- **A few things are deleted right away, and Sift says so.** Emptying the Trash is permanent. So are caches and build output — temporary files, and things like `node_modules` in the Developer page — because apps and tools rebuild them on their own, and keeping them would only keep the space taken. These are labelled "Delete for good", and Restore still notes where the space went.

## A closer look

- **Drives, Big files and Power tools.** Drives shows every connected disk, and what's on external drives and SD cards — including copies of files you already have on your computer. Big files lists the largest things you haven't opened in a while. Power tools add Explore (a map of your disk you can click into), Duplicates, Apps (uninstall an app along with the settings it leaves behind) and Developer (old build output, Docker images and simulators).

- **A weekly check-up.** Every Sunday Sift takes a quick look and sends you one notification if there's space worth freeing. It never removes anything on its own, and you can turn it off during setup or with ⌘K / Ctrl+K.

- **One permission on Mac, asked up front.** A short welcome explains why Sift needs Full Disk Access before it looks at anything, and opens the right page in System Settings. You can skip it; Sift then works with what it's allowed to see, and Home offers it again later.

## One thing worth knowing

Sift is careful about what it touches. Backup drives are never changed, and neither are external drives — Sift only shows you what's on them. Files that live only in iCloud aren't opened, so looking around never makes your Mac download them. Scans run in the background at low priority, using about a quarter of your computer's processor, so the first one can take a minute or two on a big disk.

## Works on

- **macOS** 10.15+ (Apple Silicon and Intel)
- **Windows** 10+ (x64 and ARM64)
- **Linux** (x86_64)
